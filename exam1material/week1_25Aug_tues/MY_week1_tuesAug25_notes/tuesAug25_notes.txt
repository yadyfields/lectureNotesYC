When we have an "if/then" statement,
and we know that the "if" part is true?

    ...what can we always conclude?

    *the then part is true



/////////////////////////////////////////////////////////

If I don't hear my alarm, then I will be late for class.
I am late for class.

--> What can I conclude? 

    *Nothing
    *I could have heard my alarm or not, there are other ways I could be late.

/////////////////////////////////////////////////////////

If I don't hear my alarm, then I will be late for class.
I hear my alarm.

--> What can I conclude? 

    *Nothing
    *Could have been late or not





/////////////////////////////////////////////////////////

If I don't hear my alarm, then I will be late for class.
I'm not late for class.

--> What can I conclude? 

    *either heard the alarm or didn't hear the alarm
    *Can't be the case that i didn't hear my alarm, because this would mean that i was both late and not late

    *Must be the case that i heard my alarm.

More if/then logic in programming.

*y = 17;
if (num < 4 || x >= 10) {

    y = 17;
}

--> suppose y is 17 here. What do we know?

    *Nothing. y might have been 17 before the if statement, or one of the conditions
    or both could be true.

/////////////////////////////////////////////////////////

y = 10;
if (num < 4 || x >= 10) {

    y = 17;
}

--> suppose y is 17 here. Can we conclude anything now?

    * We know one or both sides of the if was true because y changed to 17

//////////////////////////////////////////////////////////

if (num < 4 || x >= 10) {

    y = 17;
}

--> suppose y is NOT 17 here. What do we know?

    *both conditions are false,
    num >= 4 AND x < 10 (Demorgan's Law)
